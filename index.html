Questions
What does "every claim cited" actually mean?

Each finding is one row: the claim, its confidence level (established, probable, or unresolved), and the evidence record behind it — source, URL, date accessed, quoted text. You can open every source yourself. Nothing enters the report without one.

What if you find nothing?

You get the null-result list: which sources were searched and returned nothing. For a lawyer or an investor that list is a finding. We never fill a gap with a guess.

How do you avoid mixing up people with the same name?

Before anything is written, candidate matches are tested against the facts you gave us. Every look-alike that fails is excluded and listed in the report with the reason, so you see what was ruled out. A wrong merge is the worst error a background report can make, and it is the one we build against.

Do you use AI?

Collection and cross-checking are tool-assisted. Every claim is verified against its evidence by an independent reviewer, and a person approves the case before delivery.
